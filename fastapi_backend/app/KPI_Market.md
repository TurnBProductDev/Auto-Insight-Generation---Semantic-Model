# KPI Market Benchmark — Divisions vs. Saudi Arabia Market

**What this is:** each division's *actual* revenue trend from the model, benchmarked against the **best available realized Saudi market data** — the official government retail statistics, not vendor forecasts.

- **Company window:** 2026 YTD (Jan 1 → 30 Jul 2026) vs the same period 2025, from the connected model (`MIS_BASE_FILE_MONTHLY_BRAND_TB[division_name]`, measure `[revenue growth %]`). Currency: SAR, as loaded in the model.
- **Primary benchmark — GASTAT (General Authority for Statistics), Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025:** the official operating-revenue index for the trade sector. This is the gold standard: realized (not forecast), government-published, and it measures actual retail-establishment revenue — directly comparable to company revenue.
  - **Retail trade (ex. motor vehicles): +9.6% YoY** ← the headline market line used below
  - Wholesale + retail trade combined: **+7.3% YoY**
  - Retail e-commerce: **+18.4% YoY**
  - (2025 full year, realized: wholesale/retail/restaurants/hotels grew **+6.2%**.)
- **Sector color — SAMA (Saudi Central Bank) POS:** used only for realized *year-on-year* sector figures, with a caveat (see note). Weekly POS bulletins are **not** used — they are week-on-week and timing-distorted.

---

## Bottom line

**Sales are UP and beating the market — but by a modest, credible margin, not double.** Total revenue is **+12.6% YoY** against the official Saudi retail market at **+9.6%** (GASTAT Q1 2026). So the business is running **~3 points / ~1.3× ahead of the real market** — genuine outperformance, but far short of the "~2×" that the vendor forecast CAGRs (~6%) implied. Against the *best* stat, two divisions also drop **below** the market line, and LIVE KITCHEN remains the clear problem.

## Read

- **The business genuinely outperforms the official market (+12.6% vs +9.6%)** — but the margin is ~3 points, not the 2× a forecast-CAGR benchmark suggested. The honest headline is "beating a strong retail market," not "doubling it."
- **Core food (FMCG FOOD +14.4%) and discretionary (ELECTRONICS +16.8%, FOOTWEAR +15.3%, FASHION +14.5%)** are the real winners, all clearly above the market line.
- **Two divisions now fall *below* the market:** GM HOME WARE (+7.7%) and GM OTHERS (+6.3%) grow slower than the +9.6% retail market — they were flattered by the old ~5% vendor benchmark and are actually laggards. Worth attention.
- **LIVE KITCHEN (−4.1%) is the clear alarm** — declining outright while Saudi retail grows +9.6% and the restaurant/foodservice segment grows strongly. Small (0.4M) but unambiguously the wrong direction.
- **E-commerce context:** the official online-retail market grew **+18.4%** — if a meaningful share of these divisions is online, that is the tougher bar they're really competing against.

## Sector-level signals (SAMA POS, use with caution)

SAMA's Q1 2026 POS data shows the highest **year-on-year** sector growth in jewellery (~47%), **clothing & accessories (~25.9%)** and **communication (~23%)**. Treat these as *directional*, not as a like-for-like benchmark, because **card-POS growth overstates real sales growth**: Saudi Arabia is still rapidly shifting cash → card, so POS captures payment-method migration on top of actual consumption. GASTAT's retail revenue index (+9.6%) is the truer measure of real retail growth and is what the scorecard uses.

*Not publicly available as clean realized sector-YoY: food-retail, electronics, and furniture POS breakdowns — another reason the GASTAT retail index is the honest common yardstick across all divisions.*

How the score is worked out

Every product in every shop starts with 100 points, and six problems can take points off it. How many points a problem takes depends on three things: how much money is caught up in it (this counts most, half the weight), how many products it touches (a third), and how long it has been going on or how serious it is (a fifth). Damaged stock is the exception — it has no “how long” part, so money counts 70% and number of products 30%. Each problem can take at most 25 points, so no single problem can wreck the score on its own.

Products are only compared with similar products in the same shop. That way an expensive product does not look worse simply because it costs more than a cheap one.

A product that has already sold out is treated differently on purpose. It starts at 0 instead of 100, and it can go below zero if other problems pile on top. Today 19,359 products are on that path, averaging -5.5. They are labelled “Out of Stock” rather than put in one of the five bands, because a product with nothing on the shelf cannot fairly be compared with one that has stock.

The score is a summary. It is not a replacement for the actual numbers, which are in Shops, Departments and Every product.

Things to know before using these numbers

  • The score covers the five shops — ST1, ST2, ST3, ST4 and ST5. Warehouse scoring is being added to the model. Until it is there, the two warehouses appear here as stock figures only, with no score, and are never added to the shop score.
  • These are the figures on 19 August 2026, like a photograph of one moment. They are not totals for a month or a year. Only the damage figures and the shop stock line cover a stretch of time.
  • A daily copy of these figures has only just started being saved. The line under “The story” shows the shape that chart will take, not real past scores.
  • FOOTWEAR is missing its stock-cover setting, so the “more than needed” check cannot run on it at all. That is 5,583 products holding 214,739. Its score of 64.1 looks better than it should.
  • The sales missed figure of 59,051 counts only the products that sell best. There is a much larger figure in the model that counts everything, and it does not mean the same thing.
  • Two of the model’s tables are not linked up, so asking it for a single department’s score gives the whole-company number instead. Everything on this page is worked out from the main stock table, which adds up to 4,964,930 exactly.
  • 7,896 products holding 353,377 have no reorder level set, so the model cannot tell whether they are stocked correctly. They are shown as cannot tell rather than counted as fine.
  • The numbers have no currency sign. They are in the money the group reports in.

What these figures cover, and what they do not

  • This is one day’s position, not a trend. Every figure is the state of the shelves on 23 August 2026. The only history the model keeps is four weeks of weekly units and the best sales days of the last 90 days.
  • The figures cover the five shops only (ST1, ST2, ST3, ST4, ST5). The two warehouses hold stock but do not sell, so they are never added into a sales or shelf total. They appear only where a warehouse is the fix for a shop problem.
  • Money on this page is US dollars, and the model does not store it that way throughout. Stock, surplus and on-order values arrive already converted. Sales values and the missed-sales estimate arrive in the local currency, and are converted here at the same rate the model itself uses (0.27) so that everything on the page can be compared. Shelf prices are shown in the local currency and are labelled where they appear.
  • The missed-sales figure is a daily rate, not a running total. It is what the model estimates is being lost each day the shelf stays empty. It is not the amount lost so far, and it is only worked out for 1,728 of the 2,439 empty best-seller lines — a blank is not a zero, so the true figure is higher.
  • The Inventory Management report states this same figure differently. That report publishes it without the conversion applied here, so the two reports will not agree on it. The underlying column is the same one.
  • Product positions within a category cannot be compared over time. The model works out today’s position but keeps no record of past ones, so the climbers-and-fallers section is empty rather than estimated.
  • A product’s best-day list is not a sales league table. A product appears there only if yesterday beat all its own recent days, which favours smaller, less predictable lines over steady big sellers.
  • Stock is valued at what it cost, sales at what they sold for. The two are not the same measure and the page never adds one to the other.
  • Prices can be shown as low but not dated. The model holds the highest and lowest price over the window with no date attached, so the page can say a price has moved and is currently at the bottom of its range, but not when it changed.

One thing this report cannot answer yet

and what would fix it

Which products climbed or fell in their category

Not available yet

This would answer: which product just became the number one seller in its category, and which one just dropped off the top? It needs two readings taken at different times — today’s position against last month’s.

Why it is not here. The model works out each product’s position in its category as it stands today, but it keeps no record of what that position was a week or a month ago. There is nothing to compare today against. That is a gap in what is being saved, not a gap in the analysis.

What would fix it. Save that position for every product once a week into a table of its own. About a month after that starts, this section can show two lists: products newly at number one, and products that have just lost it.

Until then it stays empty rather than being filled with a stand-in figure that would read as fact. The same is true of the four weeks of weekly units the model does keep — useful for shape, too short to call a trend.

One thing this report cannot answer yet

and what would fix it

Which products climbed or fell in their category

Not available yet

This would answer: which product just became the number one seller in its category, and which one just dropped off the top? It needs two readings taken at different times — today’s position against last month’s.

Why it is not here. The model works out each product’s position in its category as it stands today, but it keeps no record of what that position was a week or a month ago. There is nothing to compare today against. That is a gap in what is being saved, not a gap in the analysis.

What would fix it. Save that position for every product once a week into a table of its own. About a month after that starts, this section can show two lists: products newly at number one, and products that have just lost it.

Until then it stays empty rather than being filled with a stand-in figure that would read as fact. The same is true of the four weeks of weekly units the model does keep — useful for shape, too short to call a trend.

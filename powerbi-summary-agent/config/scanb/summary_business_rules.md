# Summary reporting rules

These rules apply only to the Summary product. They describe how to word either
the legacy single-focus page or the balanced multi-focus page. They do not
change any calculation; numbers are always copied from supplied evidence.

## Summary structure

- In single-focus mode, explain the one deliberately selected business focus,
  keep the page about it, and lead with its exact signed movement.
- In balanced multi-focus mode, begin with `Overall Performance`, then cover all
  selected significant focus areas. Only Department, Section and Category may
  be individual focus areas. Product Group, Product and SKU may appear only as
  supporting evidence inside a selected area's deep dive.
- Keep the multi-focus page balanced: do not let one area consume the entire
  summary when other selected areas have supported material facts.

## What managers care about

- For a store or merchandise focus, managers want to know: how revenue,
  quantity and transactions changed; which smaller areas inside the focus
  contributed most (up and down); where it happened across the branches included
  in the comparison; and whether the change came mostly from selling more units
  or from a change in average revenue per item.
- When the deep dive resolved a top contributor, a leading store, or a main
  driver (see `deep_dive`), explain it in plain words after the headline figure.
  Do not invent a contributor, store or driver that the evidence does not name.

## Honest scope and language

- Only complete, reconciled evidence may be described as the full picture. When
  the deep dive marks a breakdown as partial, describe it as "the largest
  contributors returned", never as a share of the total.
- The split between selling more units and a change in average revenue per item
  is a rate or mix effect. Never call it pure price.
- Population eligibility is calculation context, not manager-facing language.
  Never write the word "comparable" in the published summary. When scope is
  useful, say "across the branches included in this comparison" or simply list
  the included branch codes. Never include an excluded or current-only store in
  a year-on-year growth statement.
- Do not claim a cause the evidence cannot prove. Say a smaller area "added" or
  "reduced" revenue; do not assert why unless the evidence states it.

## Flexible presentation

- Let the evidence determine the narrative headings, amount of detail, block
  order and charts. When chart-ready Summary evidence exists, include at least
  one useful interactive chart; the evidence and business question determine
  its type and whether more charts help. Use zero charts only when no valid
  chart dataset is available. Do not force KPI cards, a risk/success template,
  or recommended actions into the summary.
- Keep the established report theme and professional tone. Do not use emojis.
- Display month-of-year values as names (January, February, and so on), never as
  `month 1` through `month 12`.

## What this model cannot support

- **No basket level.** Never write a spend-per-visit or items-per-visit figure. The
  transaction count is a category-line count, so those levels are meaningless here
  (BR-31). Transaction, Basket Value and Basket Size **growth** is fine.
- **No branch three-lever split.** Branch transaction counts are unreliable (BR-34), so a
  branch story is told with revenue and units only.
- **No daily or weekly view.** Everything is monthly (BR-16).
- **Breakdowns do not add to the company total.** About 7.6% of revenue has no
  merchandise classification (BR-33). Say so rather than implying the breakdown is
  complete, and never show a blank area name.
